\subsection{P0: tier gain, filter gain and metric agreement}
\label{sec:results-p0}

\paragraph{Search.}
On \texttt{ugc\_strict}, every sampler improves the composite objective from 29.45 (naive upload)
to about 43--44 within 40 trials (\cref{tab:samplers}, \cref{fig:convergence}). Over five seeds,
GP-BO is the best and by far the most consistent sampler; TPE is second and random search third.
CMA-ES fails to reach the $\times2$ tier on two of five seeds within the budget: with a population
of 8, 40 trials are only five generations. The objective is reproducible, but the recipe is not:
all GP-BO seeds choose $\times2$ and a 4--6\,\% contrast increase, while grain (0--15) and the
unsharp amount (0.4--1.2) vary across seeds at the same score (43.77--43.80). The objective is flat
along these knobs.

\paragraph{Tier gain versus filter gain.}

\paragraph{Where the filter gain comes from.}
Freezing contrast and saturation (\texttt{eq}) and re-running the search yields a sharpening-only
configuration ($\times2$, unsharp 0.89, $5\times5$, no grain). It keeps 72\,\% of the composite gain
and all of the LPIPS gain, and it passes two of three judges (DISTS $+0.0035$, XPSNR $+0.52$);
SSIMULACRA2 is still lower than tier-only ($-1.74$). The colour and contrast increase, together with
the grain that the optimiser adds alongside it, thus accounts for about 30\,\% of the composite gain
and turns XPSNR and DISTS negative. At the native tier ($\times1$, no tier change), a filter-only
search finds the same $+3.0$ composite gain almost entirely from an 11\,\% contrast increase, and no
judge agrees (0/3).

\paragraph{A Goodhart matrix.}
We optimise each of four objectives in turn and judge every result with all metrics
(\cref{fig:goodhart}). No objective is free of regressions on the metrics it does not optimise.
Optimising SSIMULACRA2 comes closest (one small regression, XPSNR $-0.24$) but gains little on its
own metric ($+0.54$). Optimising LPIPS alone saturates colour, sharpening and grain and is the worst
row on every other metric (SSIMULACRA2 $-14.9$).

\paragraph{Learned pre-compensation.}
Without a trust region ($\lambda=0$, $\delta=0.15$), the learned pre-compensation moves pixels by
18--32 levels on average; on its outputs the emulator's PSNR against the real playback falls to
19--25\,dB, and real quality collapses (old\_town\_cross: 60.4 for tier-only, 39.0 with $G$). With
the trust region, $G$ is above tier-only on the objective on three of four natural clips across
seeds (not on ducks\_take\_off), and, for seed 0, on XPSNR (4/4 clips) and DISTS (3/4); SSIMULACRA2
is lower on every clip in every seed (\cref{tab:e2}). Across seeds
(3 for $G$, 5 for the parametric configuration), $G$ beats the parametric configuration only on
old\_town\_cross and only on the objective ($65.39\pm0.87$ versus $62.62\pm0.16$); on the held-out
in\_to\_tree they tie, and the parametric configuration wins on SSIMULACRA2. $G$ damages the held-out
synthetic gradient clip far more than the parametric configuration ($-10.1$ composite over
tier-only). Domain-adaptation rounds did not improve the result.

\paragraph{Graphics and text.}
The parametric configuration found on natural clips lowers the composite of the synthetic test
pattern by 11.6 relative to tier-only, mostly through CAMBI's response to the added grain, and lowers
XPSNR by 7.0. Pre-compensation therefore has to be content-dependent, which motivates P1.

All P1 numbers are gains over tier-only (or over the class-tuned sharpening-only configuration
$\theta_k$ where stated), signed so that positive is better, with 95\,\% bootstrap intervals over
clips (10{,}000 resamples); a clip's value for $G$ is its mean over three seeds.

\Cref{tab:e20} reports the sharpening-only configurations on each class's training and development

\paragraph{Loss selection (E21).}
A global $G$ trained on the 26 natural training clips (seed 0) with each perceptual loss was judged
on the seven class-A development clips against $\theta_A$. By the pre-registered rule (most judges
with a positive mean gain, SSIMULACRA2 as tie-break) the LPIPS loss was chosen: DISTS
$+0.0005$ and XPSNR $+0.22$, neither interval excluding zero, against SSIMULACRA2 $-4.23$
[$-5.10$, $-3.38$]. The DISTS-trained $G$ changed pixels about a third as much and lost less
SSIMULACRA2 ($-0.91$), but no judge favoured it.

\paragraph{Global versus specialist $G$ (E22).}
On the development split no $G$ beat $\theta_k$ in any class, and every $G$ lowered SSIMULACRA2
relative to it in every class and every seed. On class A the global $G$ was the best $G$
(SSIMULACRA2 $-4.28$, DISTS $-0.0072$, XPSNR $+0.10$ versus $\theta_A$); the specialist trained on
the 13 class-A training clips alone was worse on every judge (SSIMULACRA2 $-7.77$). On class D every
$G$ damaged graphics and animation (DISTS $-0.08$ to $-0.14$ versus tier-only). Before E23 we
committed the decisions: the global $G$ for class A; no additional confirmatory class; and the gate
table A $\to\theta_A$, B, C, D $\to$ tier-only, chosen by keeping, per class, only configurations
whose development SSIMULACRA2 and XPSNR gains over tier-only are non-negative.

\paragraph{Confirmation (E23).}
\Cref{tab:e23} and \cref{fig:e23} give the single evaluation on the confirmation clips.
\textbf{H1 is not confirmed}: on class A, $G$ is worse than $\theta_A$ on SSIMULACRA2 ($-3.52$
[$-4.26$, $-2.89$]) and DISTS ($-0.0112$ [$-0.0139$, $-0.0086$]) in all three seeds, and ties on XPSNR
($+0.01$). It nevertheless raises the composite objective by $+5.99$ over tier-only, against $+2.06$
for $\theta_A$: its gain lies in LPIPS, the metric it was trained on. H2 is supported ($G$ does not
beat $\theta_B$ on class B). H3 holds only in part: on class D the learned $G$ is worse than
tier-only on every judge, but mild sharpening is not; H3 is not testable for class C. \textbf{The
gate is accepted}: no tested class falls below tier-only on SSIMULACRA2 or XPSNR (class A:
$+0.11$, $+0.32$), and over all confirmation clips the gated system ties or beats the P0 global
configuration on SSIMULACRA2 ($+1.03$) and DISTS ($+0.0007$), while losing on XPSNR ($-0.18$
[$-0.30$, $-0.06$]).

\subsection{Why the learned pre-compensation loses}
\label{sec:results-why}

\paragraph{Not a shortage of data.}
On its own training clips, $G$ loses to $\theta_A$ by more than on unseen clips (specialist A:
SSIMULACRA2 $-10.22$ on training clips versus $-7.77$ on development clips; global $G$: $-6.21$
versus $-4.28$), and on the training clips it loses even on LPIPS, its own loss, when measured
through the real black box. This is the opposite of overfitting: the gain found through the emulator
does not survive the black box, although the emulator's pixel PSNR on $G$'s outputs is 31--36\,dB.

\paragraph{The emulator gap (E7).}
Anchoring the forward value on the black box (\cref{sec:anchored}), with everything else held fixed,
closes most of the gap (\cref{tab:e7}): with six anchors, SSIMULACRA2 versus $\theta_A$ improves from
$-4.28$ to $-0.84$ and DISTS becomes positive ($+0.0048$, interval above zero). Paired against the
P1 $G$ (same emulator and seeds), the anchored $G$ gains $+3.44$ [$+2.64$, $+4.18$] SSIMULACRA2 and
$+0.0120$ [$+0.0078$, $+0.0159$] DISTS but loses $0.60$ XPSNR. One anchor recovers about half of
that. By the pre-registered rule the estimator \emph{helps} but does not beat $\theta_A$.

\paragraph{Metric specialisation (E9).}
Starting $G$ from $\theta_A$ (the configuration placed in front of the black box, so that $G=$
identity reproduces $\theta_A$) and training it with the anchored estimator does not combine the two
strengths: $G$ improves LPIPS ($+0.0220$ [$+0.0018$, $+0.0431$]) and the composite ($+1.61$) over
$\theta_A$ but lowers SSIMULACRA2 ($-2.24$ [$-2.57$, $-1.97$], all seeds). By the pre-registered rule
it \emph{does not add} to $\theta_A$. What remains after the emulator gap is closed is a trade of
the independent judges for the training loss.

\paragraph{Cost.}
The learned route needs 300--500 black-box calls (emulator pairs plus anchors), against 195 for
$\theta_A$ (15 trials on 13 clips). On an online platform every call is an upload.

\subsection{Untuned recipes and the upload bitrate}
\label{sec:results-recipes}

\Cref{tab:e11} evaluates fixed recipes that need no tuning on all 76 tested clips (E11). Tuning adds
nothing over FFmpeg's default unsharp ($5\times5$, amount 1.0): on class A, $\theta_A$ beats it only on
DISTS, by $+0.0005$. A mild unsharp ($5\times5$, 0.5) is the only configuration in this study that
beats tier-only on all three judges on class A, with every interval above zero; it does the same on
class D and still lowers SSIMULACRA2 on class B. A gate with the mild recipe on class A and tier-only
elsewhere passes the P1 acceptance criteria on the confirmation clips and is equal to the P1 gate
within noise (SSIMULACRA2 $+0.05$, DISTS $-0.0004$, XPSNR $-0.05$). The upload bitrate matters more
than any filter: uploading at 16\,Mb/s, the rate one large platform recommends for 1440p, instead
of near-losslessly costs more SSIMULACRA2 than the best filter gains (\cref{tab:e11}).

\subsection{Platform dependence and the cost of validation}
\label{sec:results-platform}

\paragraph{Transfer between platforms (E14).}
The class-A sharpening tuned on \texttt{ugc\_strict} does not transfer to \texttt{ugc\_av1}: on the
confirmation clips SSIMULACRA2 falls below tier-only on average ($-1.18$), because two clips whose AV1
tier-only playback is already rate-starved lose heavily (controlled\_burn\_10 $-25.0$, tractor\_0
$-6.7$), while the rest are about neutral. In the other direction, the sharpening tuned on
\texttt{ugc\_av1} keeps nearly all of the native gain on \texttt{ugc\_strict} (class A composite
$+1.82$ versus $+2.02$, XPSNR $+0.37$ versus $+0.36$). Whether a recipe transfers depends on the
target platform's rate headroom. P0's full configuration, with colour, contrast and grain, lowers
SSIMULACRA2 ($-1.6$ to $-10.5$) and XPSNR ($-0.5$ to $-7.6$) on both platforms, native or moved.

\paragraph{How many clips decide a validation (E10).}
Since a recipe must be checked on each platform, we estimate how many clips such a check needs
(\cref{fig:e10}). The decision is to switch the mild recipe on if its mean gain over tier-only on
the validation clips is non-negative on SSIMULACRA2 and XPSNR; the truth is the same rule on all of a
class's clips. The two simulated platforms disagree on class A ($+0.44$ versus $-0.61$ SSIMULACRA2),
so the check is needed. A clear harm is detected with two or three clips and a clear gain with six to
eight. When the harm is concentrated in a few rate-starved clips, as on \texttt{ugc\_av1}, random
validation still errs 22\,\% of the time with ten clips; drawing half of the clips from the class's
top third by spatial information, which is known before any upload, reaches an error of at most
10\,\% with ten clips for the default recipe and sixteen for the mild one. We therefore propose
validating class A with eight to ten clips chosen this way, as tier-only and recipe: 16--20 uploads
per platform.

\paragraph{A hidden passthrough rule (E12).}
We added to \texttt{ugc\_strict} an invented rule under which small H.264 uploads ($\le720$p,
$\le2000$\,kb/s) are served without re-encoding, and let GP-BO search the upscale, the unsharp amount
and the upload bitrate. The cliff is real: at $\times1$, an upload just under the cap beats one just
over it by $+12.31$ [$+6.63$, $+16.46$] SSIMULACRA2 on class A. Serving the uploader's 720p file is
nevertheless worse than the normal 1440p route (class A $-6.44$, interval spanning zero; class B
$-26.33$), and both search seeds converge on $\times2$, unsharp $\approx1.0$ and the highest upload
bitrate. The search sampled the passthrough region in only zero or one of 30 trials, so this
experiment cannot show that it would find such a rule where it pays off; a single pair of uploads
around a suspected cap reveals whether a platform has one.
